## 2. National counts (ranges)

- Sourced anchor: BJS 2007 census counted ~900 aircraft (604 helicopters, 295
  planes) in 201 units, agencies with 100+ sworn officers only
  ([BJS](https://bjs.ojp.gov/content/pub/ascii/aullea07.txt)). Nothing newer found.
- Estimate (mine, not sourced): total US law-enforcement manned aircraft today is
  roughly 1,000 to 1,500 including small agencies and mixed with growth in
  helicopter fleets; state police and highway patrol aircraft roughly 250 to 400,
  of which fixed-wing used for traffic work is perhaps 60 to 120. Treat as order of magnitude.
- Drones now outnumber manned units by agency count
  ([Bard 2018](https://dronecenter.bard.edu/files/2018/05/CSD-Public-Safety-Drones-Update.pdf)).
- No APSA or FAA-registry analysis surfaced. A registry pull by owner name would
  firm this up cheaply.

## 3. Tracking feasibility

- Most fleets broadcast ADS-B Out (2020 mandate for airspace requiring it). Low-altitude
  fixed-wing work is often below receiver coverage, so feed gaps are expected; this
  matches SmokySignal's existing lost-contact handling.
- Hiding: FAA LADD hides registration from FAA-fed vendors; PIA issues a substitute
  ICAO address unlinked to the registry; PIA needs 1090 MHz and excludes UAT-equipped
  aircraft ([NBAA](https://nbaa.org/aircraft-operations/security/privacy/limiting-aircraft-data-displayed-ladd/)).
  Major aggregators filter police/military; ADS-B Exchange does not
  ([RTL-SDR](https://www.rtl-sdr.com/using-ads-b-exchange-to-track-police-and-military-planes-monitoring-the-george-floyd-protests/)).
  adsb.fi and adsb.lol are community aggregators and unfiltered in SmokySignal's current use.
  No source found on which state agencies actually use PIA; test per state empirically
  (hex-to-registration mismatch, or hex in the PIA block).
- Registry: FAA registry lists public-use aircraft by owner name (for example a state
  department of public safety);
  Not verified per state here. Some agencies register through a leasing or
  non-obvious entity, and some use generic department names.

## 4. Ranked shortlist

Criteria: active aerial speed enforcement, rider population/season, registry
identifiability, adjacency to WA. Evidence quality noted.

1. **Colorado.** Active, signed, a 2025 motorcycle-focused aerial op, strong
   riding culture (Rockies, Front Range). Registry check needed. Not adjacent, but best evidence.
2. **Ohio.** Largest documented program, expanding with GPS speed measurement,
   2026 activity. Long riding season, big rider base. Season short in winter.
3. **Missouri.** Fresh 2025 primary-ish data, 9 aircraft, method documented. Strong
   rider population (Ozarks).
4. **Minnesota.** Official public flight dashboard with aircraft and pilot data
   (registry-friendly), but dashboard also shows state data you could cross-check.
   Short season.
5. **Florida.** Active for decades, year-round riding, large rider base. Aircraft
   count small and public. Long-run reliability high.
6. **Montana / Idaho / Oregon (adjacent).** Adjacent and great riding, but no
   verified state-patrol aerial speed program found. Worth a registry and live-feed
   spot check before ruling in or out; Idaho and Oregon already covered as mutual aid.
7. **Pennsylvania.** Reduced but SPARE persists; big rider base. Lower confidence.
8. **South Dakota / Nebraska.** Confirmed small programs (Sturgis rally traffic is a
   specific hook for SD). Small fleets; low volume.

Skip for now: VA, MD, NY (ended), TX (not routine), AL (unused).
